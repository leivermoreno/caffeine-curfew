The curve is caffeine in your body. Each dot is a drink: sideways is when, up and down is how much. The starting two are examples.

Add a drink by pressing and holding the chart at the hour you had it, then set the amount with the slider. Tap a dot to edit or remove it.

Check any hour by sliding your finger across the chart. The line normally sits on the current time and shows what's in your body right now. While you slide it follows your finger, and when you let go it glides back to now. Drinks taken close together fan out sideways so each stays tappable; the dotted line under each one still starts at its real time.

The dashed line is the biggest drink you can still add at each hour and sleep well. It drops as bedtime gets closer.

Sensitivity sets how much caffeine you can carry into bed: 25 mg if it hits you hard, 50 mg for most people, 100 mg if coffee after dinner never bothers you. These are rules of thumb, not medical limits.

Clearance is how fast your body removes it. Half is gone in about 5 hours for most adults (fast 3.5 h, slow 7 h, very slow 10 h). Smokers clear it faster. Pregnancy, birth control pills and some medications make it much slower.

Sustained-release pills (the SR toggle) follow studies of extended-release caffeine: about a quarter absorbs like a normal drink and the rest trickles in over many hours. It peaks around 4 hours after you take it, at roughly half the height of the same dose as coffee, and the tail lasts longer, so an afternoon pill can still be releasing at night. Brands differ, and Genius doesn't publish its exact curve.

The bedtime check covers your first 3 hours of sleep, not only the moment you lie down, so a pill still releasing after bed counts.

Drink buttons can be renamed, resized or marked SR in Settings.

Weight is optional. With it, your bedtime limit scales with your body (the same milligrams make a higher concentration in a smaller body; the presets assume 70 kg / 154 lb), and two guidelines from the European Food Safety Authority appear: up to about 3 mg per kg in a single dose (never more than 200 mg), drawn as the yellow "max per dose" line, and up to about 5.7 mg per kg a day (never more than 400 mg).

Daily total is checked against 400 mg, the usual upper guideline for healthy adults.

Absorption: a drink doesn't hit your blood all at once. Regular caffeine absorbs over about an hour and peaks around 40 minutes after you drink it, at about 85% of the dose, because your body is already clearing some. Taking it with food slows this a little more, which the app doesn't model.

Things this skips: caffeine left over from the day before, decaf still has 2 to 15 mg, and chocolate and cola count too. Even if you fall asleep fine, caffeine at bedtime can cut deep sleep.
